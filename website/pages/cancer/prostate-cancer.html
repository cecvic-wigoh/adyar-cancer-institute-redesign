+ Prostate Cancer

Prostate Cancer

Most common cancer in men over 65. Early detection through PSA screening enables effective treatment with excellent outcomes.

Understanding Prostate Cancer

Prostate cancer develops in the prostate gland, a small walnut-shaped organ in men that produces seminal fluid. It is one of the most common cancers in men worldwide and is particularly prevalent in those over 65 years of age. Many prostate cancers grow slowly and may remain confined to the prostate gland, where they may not cause serious harm. However, some types are aggressive and can spread rapidly.

At Cancer Institute (WIA), our uro-oncology team provides comprehensive care for all stages of prostate cancer. From early detection through PSA (prostate-specific antigen) screening and digital rectal examination, to advanced diagnostic techniques including multiparametric MRI and targeted biopsies, we ensure accurate staging and risk stratification. Our multidisciplinary tumour board reviews each case to determine the optimal treatment strategy.

Treatment options range from active surveillance for low-risk disease to radical prostatectomy (including robotic-assisted techniques), radiation therapy (external beam and brachytherapy), and hormone therapy for advanced disease. Our approach prioritises both cancer control and preservation of quality of life, including urinary continence and sexual function. We encourage men over 50 to discuss PSA screening with their physician.

Common Symptoms

  • Difficulty urinating or frequent urination
  • Weak or interrupted urine stream
  • Blood in urine or semen
  • Pelvic discomfort or pain
  • Bone pain (especially lower back, hips, thighs)
  • Erectile dysfunction

Risk Factors

  • Age over 50 years
  • Family history of prostate cancer
  • African ancestry
  • High-fat diet
  • Obesity and sedentary lifestyle
  • BRCA2 gene mutations

Our Treatment Approach

Multidisciplinary, evidence-based treatment tailored to each patient.

Surgery

Radical prostatectomy using open or robotic-assisted techniques for localised disease, with nerve-sparing approaches to preserve urinary and sexual function wherever possible.

Radiation Therapy

External beam radiation therapy (EBRT) and brachytherapy (internal radiation) as primary treatment or post-surgical adjuvant therapy, delivered with precision to minimise side effects.

Hormone Therapy

Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) to reduce testosterone levels and slow cancer growth, used for advanced or recurrent prostate cancer, often in combination with other treatments.

Chemotherapy

Systemic chemotherapy for castration-resistant prostate cancer, using agents such as docetaxel and cabazitaxel to control disease progression and improve outcomes.

Active Surveillance

Careful monitoring of low-risk prostate cancers with regular PSA tests, imaging, and biopsies, avoiding unnecessary treatment while ensuring timely intervention if the cancer progresses.
